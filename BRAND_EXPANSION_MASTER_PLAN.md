## One brand. Three dimensions. One philosophy.

**Status:** Proposed — research and planning only; no new division is approved for commercial launch.

**Repository:** eboxont-code/skyco  
**Related plans:** README.md, MASTER_PLAN.md, roadmap.md and AGENTS.md

## 0. Purpose, scope and guardrails

This document is the master checklist for evaluating and, only after approval, building SkyCo as a broader brand with three connected dimensions:

1. **SKYCO Technology** — the existing digital product ecosystem and its planned Android client.
2. **SKYCO Lifestyle** — a proposed physical-products division, beginning with clothing/apparel only if the name, demand, economics and operating model are validated.
3. **SKYCO Community** — a proposed content and community dimension focused on practical encouragement, personal growth, wellbeing and helping people move forward.

The shared philosophy is:

> **SKYCO — As Above, So Below.**  
> One brand. Three dimensions. One philosophy.

The intended promise is to make everyday life better, support progress and give people practical tools, useful products and meaningful encouragement. This is a strategic direction to validate, not a claim that the new divisions already exist.

### 0.1 What this plan does not do

- It does not replace, pause or rewrite the current website-to-Android delivery plan in MASTER_PLAN.md.
- It does not authorise changes to the live website, current products, Android scope or existing routes.
- It does not establish that SKYCO is legally available for clothing, retail or any other new goods/services.
- It does not choose the final clothing style, audience, gender range, supplier country, manufacturing method, price point or launch date.
- It does not authorise stock purchases, production, paid samples, trademark filings, paid software, paid advertising, contractors, legal fees or other expenditure.
- It does not authorise health, medical, therapeutic, sustainability, ethical-sourcing or performance claims that cannot be substantiated.
- It does not make SKYCO Community a medical, mental-health, financial-advice or crisis-support service.

### 0.2 Non-negotiable operating rules

- [ ] **Default additional budget is AUD $0.** Use research, planning and existing/free tools first.
- [ ] Before any cost is incurred, write down the amount, purpose, alternatives and expected benefit, then obtain explicit founder approval.
- [ ] No paid ads, subscriptions, paid APIs, paid design tools, paid e-commerce plans, paid freelancers, samples, inventory, manufacturing deposits or trademark applications without approval.
- [ ] Do not promise a free physical launch: product samples, fulfilment, payment processing, returns, compliance work and manufacturing can create real costs.
- [ ] Do not take customer money or publish a product for sale until name clearance, product compliance, delivery capability, consumer-law requirements and unit economics pass their gates.
- [ ] Do not buy inventory based only on likes, compliments, survey answers or social-media engagement.
- [ ] Do not claim that a supplier, factory, fabric, garment or business is ethical, sustainable, locally made, premium or certified without evidence.
- [ ] Do not mix customer marketing permissions between Technology, Lifestyle and Community. Obtain and record the appropriate consent for each purpose.
- [ ] Do not collect more personal data than a specific feature needs.
- [ ] Keep existing Technology services useful and independently maintained. A new Lifestyle or Community feature must not destabilise the live tools.
- [ ] Keep every task unchecked until evidence exists. Record the evidence beside the task, in a PR, issue, decision log or test report.
- [ ] Mark founder decisions and external dependencies as blocked rather than guessing.
- [ ] Treat this plan as a living backlog. Record decisions, assumptions, risks and changes; do not silently rewrite the strategy.

### 0.3 Priority key

- **P0 — Gate/blocker:** must be resolved before public use of the proposed apparel name, accepting orders or launching the relevant activity.
- **P1 — Before pilot/launch:** must be completed before the first paid pilot or public launch.
- **P2 — Build and improve:** valuable after the first validation gate or pilot.
- **P3 — Later scale:** only after evidence of repeat demand and sustainable operations.

## 2. Brand architecture and strategy

### 2.1 Parent brand and relationship between divisions — P1

- [ ] Confirm SKYCO remains the parent identity and “As Above, So Below” remains the guiding philosophy.
- [ ] Approve the brand architecture: SKYCO as parent; Technology, Lifestyle and Community as distinct divisions.
- [ ] Define what the three divisions have in common: purpose, attitude, quality expectations, tone and visual identity.
- [ ] Define what each division does not do, so the parent brand does not become vague or incoherent.
- [ ] Define whether each division is a product line, operating unit, trading name or separate legal entity. Obtain accounting/legal advice before structuring entities.
- [ ] Keep the existing Technology division's product names and live routes intact unless a separately approved change is made.
- [ ] Define whether Lifestyle will initially be a landing page, a store under the existing domain, a separate commerce domain or another architecture. Do not register a new domain until name and architecture checks pass.
- [ ] Define how Community relates to the product business: editorial content, events, member participation, support resources or a combination.
- [ ] Write a one-page brand architecture map showing the parent brand, divisions, products, channels and customer relationships.
- [ ] Create a decision log for brand architecture decisions and their rationale.

### 2.2 Mission, vision and promise — P1

- [ ] Draft a one-sentence parent-brand purpose.
- [ ] Draft a concise mission statement that connects practical technology, useful lifestyle products and community.
- [ ] Draft a future vision for what SkyCo should become over 3–5 years.
- [ ] Define a clear customer promise that can be demonstrated through real product/service behaviour.
- [ ] Define the meaning of “As Above, So Below” in plain language for customers.
- [ ] Decide how much the philosophy is expressed publicly versus used internally as a guiding principle.
- [ ] Define 3–5 brand values with observable behaviours rather than generic adjectives.
- [ ] Define the emotional outcome SkyCo should create without promising that every customer will experience the same result.
- [ ] Define a short description for the parent brand.
- [ ] Define a short description for Technology, Lifestyle and Community separately.
- [ ] Test the descriptions with people outside the project to check whether the brand is immediately understandable.
- [ ] Keep the approved brand story consistent across the website, app, packaging, garments, social profiles and business documents.

### 2.3 Positioning and differentiation — P1

- [ ] Research whether the best position is premium streetwear, everyday essentials, active lifestyle, outdoor/adventure apparel or another evidence-backed niche.
- [ ] Do not choose “premium” solely because of visual style; define measurable product reasons customers would pay more.
- [ ] Identify the specific customer problem or desire the apparel offer serves.
- [ ] Write a positioning statement: target customer, category, distinctive benefit, proof and alternative customers use today.
- [ ] Identify at least three credible differentiators that can be delivered consistently.
- [ ] Avoid competing only on price, generic motivational slogans or a logo printed on a blank garment.
- [ ] Decide whether the brand should be understated, expressive, technical, performance-led, casual or a deliberate combination.
- [ ] Define the level of visible branding customers should expect: prominent graphic, subtle mark, embroidery, woven label or collection artwork.
- [ ] Decide how the clothing story links to the wider SkyCo purpose without forcing a connection that customers do not understand.
- [ ] Test the positioning against competitor products, reviews and customer interviews.
- [ ] Write a positioning decision record and list the assumptions still unproven.

## 3. Customer and market research

### 3.1 Define target markets — P1

- [ ] Confirm the first market: Australia, Nepal, or another explicitly selected market. Australia is the initial regulatory research baseline only, not a final market decision.
- [ ] Decide whether the first customer segment is local, national or international.
- [ ] Define customer segments by needs, style, purchase behaviour, price tolerance and use case rather than unsupported demographic stereotypes.
- [ ] Decide whether the first range is men's, women's, unisex or a carefully designed inclusive range.
- [ ] Define intended age range only after considering fit, sizing, safety and market evidence.
- [ ] Identify whether the first buyer is purchasing for themselves, as a gift or for a community/team.
- [ ] Map climate and seasonality by target market before selecting fabrics and product types.
- [ ] Research shipping expectations, duties and return friction in each proposed market.
- [ ] Document which markets are expressly out of scope for the first release.

### 3.2 Customer discovery — P1

- [ ] Write a neutral interview guide that does not lead participants toward liking the brand.
- [ ] Identify 20–30 potential customers across the candidate segments for initial discovery. This is a proposed research target, not a market statistic.
- [ ] Interview customers about what they buy now, how often, their preferred fit, quality problems, price expectations and what would make them switch.
- [ ] Ask what they recently purchased, not only what they say they might buy.
- [ ] Capture recurring language customers use to describe the style and quality they want.
- [ ] Separate friends/family encouragement from evidence from independent potential customers.
- [ ] Record consent and avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive personal information.
- [ ] Summarise interview patterns without exposing participants' private details.
- [ ] Identify the strongest unmet need and the customer group with the clearest buying intent.
- [ ] Run a second round of interviews if the results are mixed or the concept changes.

### 3.3 Competitor and category research — P1

- [ ] Build a competitor list with at least 15 brands across direct, adjacent, premium and value segments.
- [ ] Include Australian brands and international brands relevant to the intended niche.
- [ ] Include same-name/near-name businesses as a separate legal and brand-confusion review, not simply as style competitors.
- [ ] Record product category, price, fit, fabrics, design language, product photography, delivery promise, returns policy and customer reviews.
- [ ] Identify repeated complaints about quality, sizing, fading, shrinkage, delivery, customer service and price/value.
- [ ] Compare product detail pages and size guides to identify the information customers need before buying.
- [ ] Compare social content and community activity without copying another brand's creative work.
- [ ] Identify gaps SkyCo can credibly serve rather than claiming that competitors have no differentiation.
- [ ] Build a positioning matrix showing price versus quality/identity, with sources and date checked.
- [ ] Summarise competitor research in a concise market brief.
- [ ] Recheck competitors immediately before a launch decision because their products and pricing change.

### 3.4 Demand validation before investment — P0/P1

- [ ] Create 2–3 distinct concept directions after brand and audience research.
- [ ] Prepare no-cost mockups or simple concept boards using existing/free tools.
- [ ] Label mockups clearly as concepts, not available products.
- [ ] Test concept comprehension with people who are not already invested in SkyCo.
- [ ] Use a free, consent-based expression-of-interest page only if it can be built without paid commitments and without implying that a product is ready.
- [ ] Keep marketing consent separate from a request for product research feedback.
- [ ] Record where each lead came from and the exact permission granted.
- [ ] Define a meaningful validation threshold before running the test: qualified interest, target-customer mix, willingness to pay at a viable price and evidence of purchase intent.
- [ ] Do not count followers, likes, views, giveaway entries or vague compliments as sales validation.
- [ ] Test willingness to pay with realistic price ranges that account for landed cost, fulfilment, tax, fees and returns.
- [ ] Decide whether a preorder test is appropriate only after supplier cost, delivery dates, refund handling and legal requirements are known.
- [ ] Do not accept deposits or preorders until all order, refund, consumer-law and fulfilment obligations are ready.
- [ ] Write a validation report with evidence, sample limitations, result and go/no-go recommendation.
- [ ] Stop or revise the concept if customers do not show credible demand at a sustainable price.

## 4. SKYCO Lifestyle — clothing range strategy

### 4.1 Choose the first product category — P1

- [ ] Compare heavyweight T-shirts, everyday T-shirts, hoodies, sweatshirts, caps, activewear, outerwear and other relevant categories.
- [ ] Compare premium streetwear, minimalist essentials, outdoor/adventure, performance and casual lifestyle concepts.
- [ ] Evaluate climate suitability for the first market and the season in which a first drop could realistically ship.
- [ ] Evaluate technical complexity, sizing risk, quality-control burden, supplier options and return likelihood for each product.
- [ ] Choose one hero product category for the initial pilot rather than launching a broad catalogue.
- [ ] Define a small capsule range only after the hero category is validated.
- [ ] Decide whether the first collection is primarily fit-led, fabric-led, design-led or message-led.
- [ ] Decide the intended use cases: everyday wear, travel, training, outdoors, work/casual or other.
- [ ] Write the range architecture and explain why each SKU exists.
- [ ] Do not add accessories just to make the launch look larger.

### 4.2 Customer and fit specification — P1

- [ ] Define fit intent for each garment: regular, relaxed, oversized, tailored, performance or other.
- [ ] Define a measurement chart with garment measurements, not only generic body-size labels.
- [ ] Define tolerances for each key measurement with the manufacturer.
- [ ] Decide size range using customer evidence and sample grading, not guesswork.
- [ ] Test fit on a suitably varied sample group with permission and a documented feedback method.
- [ ] Check fit consistency across sizes, not only on one model.
- [ ] Create a fit guide explaining intended ease, length, rise, sleeve, shoulder and other relevant dimensions.
- [ ] Define inclusive fit principles and avoid misleading claims about universal fit.
- [ ] Decide how size exchanges will work operationally and financially.
- [ ] Check fit again after fabric, wash or supplier changes.

### 4.3 Fabric, trims and construction — P1

- [ ] Define fabric requirements for each concept: fibre composition, weight/GSM, knit or weave, stretch, hand feel, drape and intended climate.
- [ ] Define acceptable shrinkage and dimensional stability after washing.
- [ ] Define acceptable colourfastness for washing, rubbing and light exposure where relevant.
- [ ] Define seam, stitch density, neckline, cuffs, hems, drawcords, zips and reinforcement requirements.
- [ ] Define print, embroidery, applique or other decoration durability requirements.
- [ ] Decide label type and placement: brand label, size label, fibre/content information where applicable, care label and origin description as appropriate.
- [ ] Define a packaging specification that protects the garment without unnecessary material.
- [ ] Obtain written material and construction specifications from any supplier before approving production.
- [ ] Verify material and performance claims against supplier evidence and product testing.
- [ ] Avoid unsupported words such as organic, recycled, carbon-neutral, non-toxic, ethically made or sustainable.
- [ ] Store the final technical pack and approved artwork/version with each SKU.

### 4.4 Design and collection development — P1/P2

- [ ] Develop a visual direction for the first capsule from the approved parent identity.
- [ ] Explore typography, iconography, placements and garment silhouettes.
- [ ] Check all logo and graphic concepts for third-party IP conflicts.
- [ ] Obtain written ownership/licence terms for any commissioned artwork, photography, fonts or design assets.
- [ ] Keep artwork editable and store final production-ready vector files.
- [ ] Develop a colour palette tied to available fabric and dye options.
- [ ] Confirm colour references with suppliers rather than relying on screen appearance.
- [ ] Develop front, back, sleeve, label and packaging mockups.
- [ ] Review visual impact at actual garment scale, not only on a large screen.
- [ ] Ensure each design remains recognisably SkyCo without using another brand's distinctive look.
- [ ] Create a design review scorecard for clarity, originality, wearability, production feasibility and cost.
- [ ] Approve only designs that pass both brand review and supplier feasibility review.

## 5. Supplier, manufacturing and sourcing strategy

### 5.1 Choose the production model — P1

- [ ] Compare print-on-demand, blank-garment decoration, private label, cut-and-sew, local manufacture and preorder-funded production.
- [ ] Compare the true landed cost and quality control of each model.
- [ ] Compare minimum order quantities, setup charges, sample charges, lead times and reorder constraints.
- [ ] Compare control over fit, fabric, colour, labels and packaging.
- [ ] Compare returns, defective stock, lost parcels and unsold-stock exposure.
- [ ] Assess whether a print-on-demand model is acceptable for the intended quality and brand position.
- [ ] Assess whether preorders could work without misleading delivery promises or taking unmanageable cash-flow risk.
- [ ] Assess whether a small initial batch is financially safer than custom manufacturing.
- [ ] Document the selected model and why alternatives were rejected.
- [ ] Do not select a factory based solely on its quoted unit price.

### 5.2 Supplier discovery and due diligence — P1

- [ ] Build a supplier longlist for the selected product and manufacturing model.
- [ ] Compare suppliers in Australia and other suitable regions only after market, duty, quality and delivery research.
- [ ] Verify supplier legal identity, trading history, location and references.
- [ ] Confirm whether the supplier is the factory, an agent, a trading company or a print-on-demand intermediary.
- [ ] Confirm where fabric, cutting, sewing, printing, embroidery, finishing and packing occur.
- [ ] Request written MOQ, price breaks, sample price, setup fees, production lead time and payment terms.
- [ ] Request fibre composition, GSM, construction, colour and care information.
- [ ] Request relevant test reports, certifications and factory audit evidence where claims or product risks make them relevant.
- [ ] Ask how defects, rejected goods, late delivery and rework are handled.
- [ ] Ask whether production can be repeated consistently and how a batch is identified.
- [ ] Check supplier references and recent customer feedback independently.
- [ ] Verify bank/payment details through an independent channel before transferring funds.
- [ ] Assess supplier business continuity, communication, quality systems and capacity.
- [ ] Review working conditions and supply-chain transparency; do not advertise ethical sourcing without support.
- [ ] Record supplier due-diligence evidence and unresolved risks.
- [ ] Reject suppliers that refuse basic specifications, traceability, samples or written terms.

### 5.3 Samples and quality assurance — P0 before production

- [ ] Prepare a written tech pack and purchase specification before requesting a development sample.
- [ ] Approve a sample budget only through the explicit cost-approval process.
- [ ] Inspect sample measurements against the size spec.
- [ ] Conduct wash and dry tests using intended care instructions.
- [ ] Check shrinkage, twisting, pilling, colour bleed, fading, seam opening and print/embroidery durability.
- [ ] Check hand feel, comfort, breathability and transparency where relevant.
- [ ] Check label attachment, label accuracy and packaging.
- [ ] Photograph and record every sample issue.
- [ ] Send one consolidated correction list to the supplier.
- [ ] Re-test corrected samples rather than assuming a written promise resolved the issue.
- [ ] Keep a sealed or clearly identified approved reference sample.
- [ ] Create a signed quality-control checklist for production batches.
- [ ] Define acceptable defect levels and a remedy process in the supplier agreement.
- [ ] Define who pays for defective, late or non-conforming units.
- [ ] Keep batch, supplier and shipment traceability records.

### 5.4 Supplier agreements and IP — P1

- [ ] Put specifications, quantity, price, delivery date, quality requirements and payment milestones in writing.
- [ ] Define ownership of patterns, artwork, labels, moulds, print files and other paid-for production assets.
- [ ] Define confidentiality and permitted use of SkyCo designs.
- [ ] Prohibit unauthorised overproduction or resale of branded overruns where contractually possible.
- [ ] Define defect inspection, rejection, rework, replacement and refund remedies.
- [ ] Define shipping terms, insurance, title/risk transfer and responsibility for customs.
- [ ] Define cancellation and delay remedies.
- [ ] Define use of subcontractors and require disclosure of material production changes.
- [ ] Have high-risk or material agreements reviewed by an appropriate professional before signing.

## 6. Commercial model, pricing and financial controls

### 6.1 Build the financial model — P0 before any paid order

- [ ] Build a SKU-level cost model with editable assumptions.
- [ ] Include garment/fabric cost, decoration, labels, packaging, freight, duties, import GST where applicable, customs/broker charges, warehousing, pick-and-pack and delivery.
- [ ] Include payment processing, platform costs, returns, exchanges, defects, discounts, chargebacks and customer support time.
- [ ] Separate fixed costs, variable costs and one-off setup costs.
- [ ] Model prices including and excluding GST where relevant to the selected business/tax status.
- [ ] Calculate contribution per order after variable costs.
- [ ] Calculate gross margin and contribution margin using clearly defined formulas.
- [ ] Calculate break-even units and cash required before first revenue.
- [ ] Model best, base and downside cases.
- [ ] Stress-test higher freight, exchange rates, duties, returns and supplier defects.
- [ ] Model discounting without assuming full-price sales for every unit.
- [ ] Model unsold inventory and markdown exposure.
- [ ] Include the value of founder time in management decisions even if no cash wage is initially paid.
- [ ] Define a maximum loss/cash exposure for any pilot.
- [ ] Define a stop-loss rule for supplier or campaign spending.
- [ ] Do not set retail prices until the cost model and target customer willingness-to-pay evidence are both available.
- [ ] Record assumptions and sources for every externally derived cost.
- [ ] Recalculate the model when supplier quotes, exchange rates, freight or taxes change.

### 6.2 Business structure, accounts and tax — P0 before trading

- [ ] Confirm the legal seller of record and whether Lifestyle is operated by the existing entity or another appropriate structure.
- [ ] Check existing ABN, entity, business-name and GST circumstances before creating duplicate registrations.
- [ ] Confirm whether a separate registered business name is required for the way the division will trade.
- [ ] Understand that registering a business name is not the same as obtaining trade mark rights.
- [ ] Confirm who owns the apparel stock, customer contracts, brand assets and sales proceeds.
- [ ] Confirm bookkeeping categories for stock, freight, refunds, cost of goods sold and marketing.
- [ ] Confirm inventory accounting method and records with an accountant.
- [ ] Monitor GST turnover. The general Australian GST registration threshold is AUD $75,000 in GST turnover, subject to the applicable rules; verify current obligations against the whole relevant enterprise and seek tax advice rather than treating the clothing division in isolation.
- [ ] Confirm whether imported goods create GST, duty, declaration or other obligations for the selected shipment and supplier arrangement.
- [ ] Set a monthly financial review and reconciliation process.
- [ ] Do not collect GST from customers unless the business is registered and entitled to do so.
- [ ] Keep business and personal money appropriately separated under the chosen structure.

### 6.3 Pricing strategy — P1

- [ ] Compare target customer willingness to pay with real competitor pricing.
- [ ] Decide price architecture across entry, core and premium items if multiple tiers are justified.
- [ ] Decide whether delivery is charged separately, threshold-based or included in product prices.
- [ ] Calculate shipping subsidy impact before advertising free shipping.
- [ ] Define an exchange/returns allowance in the model.
- [ ] Decide whether launch offers are needed and how they affect perceived value.
- [ ] Avoid perpetual discounts that undermine the value proposition.
- [ ] Ensure any Was/Now or recommended retail price claims are genuine and supportable.
- [ ] Document the final price rationale and the margin at the approved price.

## 7. Legal, compliance and customer trust — Australia baseline

This section is a task list, not legal or tax advice. Recheck current official requirements before supply, import or launch.

### 7.1 Trade marks, business names and intellectual property — P0

- [ ] Complete the name-conflict investigation in Section 1 before public apparel use.
- [ ] Confirm the correct trade mark applicant/owner and whether ownership should sit with the existing entity or another entity.
- [ ] Confirm the goods/services specification with a trade mark professional where needed.
- [ ] Review Class 25 for clothing, footwear and headwear; assess any other classes only against the real business model.
- [ ] Search Australia and each planned export market.
- [ ] Decide whether to seek protection for the word mark, logo or both.
- [ ] Confirm ownership/licences for all apparel artwork, icons, patterns, photos, videos, fonts and copy.
- [ ] Keep a register of licences, expiry dates, usage limits and attribution obligations.
- [ ] Obtain written model releases and photographer/creator permissions for commercial campaign use.
- [ ] Do not copy competitor artwork, graphics, photos or trade dress.
- [ ] Record final IP clearance and ownership evidence.

### 7.2 Product descriptions, labels and claims — P0

- [ ] Review the Australian care-labelling mandatory standard for every proposed garment/textile category.
- [ ] Confirm whether the product is covered by the Consumer Goods (Care Labelling) Information Standard 2023 or an exception.
- [ ] Provide compliant care instructions in English and/or the permitted international care symbols where the standard applies.
- [ ] Verify fibre/composition, fabric weight, country of origin and performance information with the supplier before publishing.
- [ ] Determine which labels and product descriptions are legally required for each garment.
- [ ] Do not assume a voluntary fibre or quality claim is mandatory or sufficient; verify the specific product rule.
- [ ] Ensure product imagery and descriptions match the actual garment, colour, fit and fabric.
- [ ] Substantiate claims such as heavyweight, organic, recycled, breathable, durable, moisture-wicking, made in Australia or ethically made.
- [ ] Avoid unsupported environmental claims; ensure the overall impression, not only the literal words, is accurate.
- [ ] Avoid implying that a garment is Australian-made merely because the brand is Australian.
- [ ] Keep supplier invoices, certificates, test reports and claim substantiation for the products sold.

### 7.3 Product safety and supply chain — P0

- [ ] Check the ACCC Product Safety mandatory standards and bans relevant to the selected products and intended customers.
- [ ] Confirm whether any child/infant product is in scope; do not launch children's garments without a separate safety and compliance review.
- [ ] Source products that are safe and fit for purpose.
- [ ] Assess relevant hazards and appropriate testing before supply.
- [ ] Establish a complaint and incident escalation process.
- [ ] Record batch identifiers, supplier, production dates and affected orders.
- [ ] Prepare a product withdrawal/recall contact and response plan.
- [ ] Understand mandatory reporting duties for a death, serious injury or serious illness associated with supplied consumer goods.
- [ ] Ensure contracts do not assume that the supplier removes SkyCo's own responsibilities as an Australian supplier/importer.
- [ ] Maintain records to demonstrate reasonable product-safety due diligence.

### 7.4 Australian Consumer Law and ecommerce — P0 before taking orders

- [ ] Prepare clear terms of sale and ensure they do not attempt to remove consumer guarantees.
- [ ] Write a returns/refunds policy that distinguishes faulty goods and other consumer-guarantee remedies from optional change-of-mind returns.
- [ ] Do not use a blanket “no refunds” statement.
- [ ] Define a process for complaints, faulty items, wrong items, lost deliveries and delayed shipments.
- [ ] State realistic dispatch windows and shipping estimates.
- [ ] Disclose total price, delivery charges, taxes and any other unavoidable charges before purchase.
- [ ] Ensure product claims and reviews are not misleading or manipulated.
- [ ] Ensure stock availability and preorder status are accurate.
- [ ] Provide the business identity and contact details customers need to contact the seller.
- [ ] Ensure checkout clearly identifies what the customer is buying and when payment is taken.
- [ ] Test order confirmation, cancellation handling, refunds and payment failure.
- [ ] Review privacy obligations and payment-provider security for the actual business model.
- [ ] Confirm whether the Privacy Act applies to the entity; do not assume the small-business exemption applies without checking all relevant exceptions and related-entity circumstances.
- [ ] Publish a privacy notice consistent with actual data collection, use, sharing, retention and deletion.
- [ ] Keep only the customer information needed to fulfil orders, support customers and meet legal obligations.

### 7.5 Marketing, email and social compliance — P0 before campaigns

- [ ] Collect valid permission before sending marketing emails, SMS or other commercial electronic messages.
- [ ] Keep an auditable record of consent, including when and how it was obtained.
- [ ] Identify the sender and include correct contact details in commercial electronic messages.
- [ ] Include an easy-to-use unsubscribe facility and action unsubscribe requests within the required timeframe.
- [ ] Do not add existing Technology users to Lifestyle marketing lists merely because they use a SkyCo tool.
- [ ] Do not treat product-research participation as marketing consent.
- [ ] Create separate permission choices for Technology updates, Lifestyle offers and Community communications.
- [ ] Obtain permission before publishing customer photos, reviews, testimonials or user-generated content.
- [ ] Disclose paid, gifted or affiliate relationships where relevant.
- [ ] Review influencer content and campaign claims for accuracy.
- [ ] Do not make health, body-image, financial or wellbeing promises that the product cannot substantiate.

### 7.6 Imports and international sales — P1/P2

- [ ] Determine the exact import model: SkyCo importer of record, supplier-delivered DDP, customer-paid duties or local production.
- [ ] Confirm tariff classification for each imported garment using the current Australian Working Tariff.
- [ ] Check country-of-origin and trade-description requirements for the actual products and shipment.
- [ ] Obtain a landed-cost estimate from a licensed customs broker or official source before a significant import.
- [ ] Include applicable duty, import GST, freight, insurance, brokerage and import-processing charges in the cost model.
- [ ] Confirm whether the shipment requires an import declaration and what evidence the importer must hold.
- [ ] Check any restricted-goods, packaging or market-specific requirements before shipping.
- [ ] For overseas sales, assess consumer-law, tax, privacy, labelling, import and returns obligations in each destination market.
- [ ] Do not offer worldwide shipping until the markets and obligations have been selected and tested.

## 8. Brand identity and creative system

### 8.1 Parent identity — P1

## 17. Recommended execution order

Do not work down every section at once. Follow this sequence to avoid wasted effort:

1. **Protect the name first:** investigate SKYCO apparel use, Australian business-name signals, Australian/international trade marks and legal clearance.
2. **Define the brand:** lock parent/division architecture and the meaning of the philosophy.
3. **Research the customer:** market, competitors, customer interviews and price tolerance.
4. **Validate the offer for free:** concept boards, mockups and permission-based interest research.
5. **Choose one product:** category, fit, size, fabric and product specification.
6. **Compare suppliers:** due diligence, written quotes, landed cost and sample plan.
7. **Prove the economics:** SKU margin, cash exposure, returns and downside scenario.
8. **Complete compliance:** labels, product safety, ACL, tax, import, privacy and marketing.
9. **Build only what is needed:** store, checkout, fulfilment and support.
10. **Run a controlled pilot:** obtain explicit founder approval and measure actual results.
11. **Decide whether to scale:** stop, improve, repeat or expand based on evidence.
